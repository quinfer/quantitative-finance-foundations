# Quantitative Finance Foundations

A structured curriculum for finance students developing rigorous quantitative capabilities in empirical asset pricing, econometric inference, and systematic trading strategies.

## About This Resource

This repository provides curated guidance for students transitioning from traditional finance education to quantitative research and implementation. It emphasises methodological foundations over superficial familiarity with tools, focusing on resources that combine theoretical rigour with empirical validation and practical implementability.

**Maintained by**: Prof Barry Quinn, Ulster University Business School, Centre for Finance and Responsible Technology

**Selection Criteria**: Resources included here meet standards of mathematical rigour, empirical validation, and practical implementation. Preference is given to works that have demonstrated lasting value over trendy but untested approaches. This curriculum reflects methods taught in advanced quantitative finance programmes and used in systematic investment research.

## Why This Curriculum Exists

Many finance students encounter quantitative methods through fragmented sources—a machine learning course here, a derivatives pricing module there—without developing the integrated foundations needed for rigorous research or professional practice. This curriculum addresses that gap by providing a coherent progression through:

- **Mathematical foundations** enabling formal reasoning about uncertainty and dynamics

- **Econometric methods** for causal inference and prediction with financial data

- **Market microstructure** understanding how markets actually function

- **Implementation practices** building reproducible, testable research code

- **Advanced applications** applying these foundations to real problems

The resources here aim for the rigour of López de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* and Dixon et al.'s *Machine Learning in Finance* whilst remaining accessible to motivated students with solid undergraduate preparation.

## Recommended Progression

Students should develop capabilities in parallel rather than attempting sequential mastery:

1. **Start immediately** with Python implementation whilst building mathematical foundations

2. **Develop concurrently** time series econometrics and statistical learning methods

3. **Ground everything** in understanding of market microstructure and institutional reality

4. **Maintain throughout** rigorous software practices and reproducibility standards

The worst approach is attempting to "complete" mathematical preparation before coding or delaying implementation until theory is mastered. Quantitative finance requires iterative development of theory, methods, and implementation skills.

## Repository Structure

- **`resources/`**: Detailed guidance on essential readings organised by theme

- **`implementations/`**: Documented Python examples demonstrating rigorous practice

- **`papers/`**: Essential research papers with annotations and context

- **`exercises/`**: Implementation tasks building practical capabilities

*Note*: The `implementations/` and `exercises/` directories are under active development; any code added there should be treated as experimental until explicitly documented as tested.

## Core Philosophy

This curriculum reflects several principles:

**Empirical evidence leads**: Methods here have demonstrated value through application to real problems, not just theoretical elegance or novelty.

**Rigour is non-negotiable**: Quantitative finance requires understanding assumptions, testing them systematically, and stating limitations clearly. Resources that hand-wave methodology or overstate conclusions are excluded.

**Implementation matters**: Theory without implementation capability proves useless in practice. This curriculum emphasises building working systems, not just understanding concepts.

**Sustainability over trends**: Methods grounded in econometrics, mathematical finance, and statistical learning persist across market cycles. Industry buzzwords (FinTech, AI-powered trading, blockchain solutions) come and go.

## Prerequisites

Students engaging with this curriculum should have:

- **Mathematics**: Multivariate calculus, linear algebra, probability theory at undergraduate level

- **Statistics**: Hypothesis testing, regression analysis, maximum likelihood estimation

- **Programming**: Basic Python competency (data structures, control flow, functions)

- **Finance**: Understanding of asset pricing, portfolio theory, derivatives fundamentals

If these prerequisites aren't met, address gaps before attempting advanced quantitative methods. Poor foundations guarantee future difficulties.

## How to Use This Resource

1. **Assess your starting point**: Review prerequisites honestly. Identify gaps.

2. **Follow the progression**: Start with mathematical foundations and econometric methods concurrently.

3. **Implement everything**: Reading without coding builds false confidence. Implement examples, reproduce results, extend methods.

4. **Document your work**: Maintain a research journal noting what works, what fails, and why.

5. **Engage critically**: Question methods, test assumptions, identify limitations. Rigorous scepticism distinguishes researchers from implementers.
